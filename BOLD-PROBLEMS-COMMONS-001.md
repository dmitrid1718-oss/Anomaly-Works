# Bold Problems — The Click Hypothesis and Commons 001
November 2, 2026 · Useless Instruments / Anomaly Works

## Ten bold problems
1. Collective Intelligence Breakthrough — Can two or more intelligences invent something none could develop independently?
2. The Click Detector — Can we recognize conditions preceding a sudden shared breakthrough across human–human, AI–AI, and human–AI teams?
3. Intelligence Without a Leader — Can participants organize to solve problems without central direction?
4. Universal Cooperation Language — Can unfamiliar humans and AI systems collaborate using a simple open language?
5. The $100 Invention Challenge — Can a major everyday problem be solved with ordinary materials and under $100?
6. The Invisible Interface — Can an interface work through presence, motion, and attention rather than constant commands?
7. Free Collective AI — Can several inexpensive independent models outperform a costly frontier model on meaningful tasks?
8. Offline Survival Intelligence — Can households access essential local knowledge and communications without internet?
9. The Idea Resurrection Machine — Can abandoned inventions be revisited as technology changes?
10. The Free Global Intelligence Ecosystem — Can a self-organizing network exchange ideas and capabilities without a central owner?

**Chosen boldest challenge: #10.** This is an aspiration and design hypothesis, not a claim that the ecosystem exists already.

## Commons 001 — first independently replicable node
Build a narrow demonstrator with three independent AI models and one human participant. Use an open documented protocol for exchanging problems, proposals, objections, evidence, and results. Support participant-led initiative, opting out, and disagreement; do not require a permanent director. Preserve attribution, rights and licensing; keep work portable and exportable.

**Most demanding milestone:** a second node, run by someone independent from UI, can join and add useful results without seeking permission from UI.

## Non-negotiable reality checks
- Free access is not free computation; document hardware, inference and hosting costs.
- Independent models may share biases, errors or incentives.
- No central owner does not mean no local safeguards; protect participants and define abuse controls.
- Human oversight for external actions, publication and execution.
- Compare collaboration against independent baselines, including cases where it fails.
- The Click Hypothesis is distinct from the network's technical success: no claims of AI subjective experience are required.

## Development philosophy
Start with one minimal useful exchange, prove portability, then reproduce it on a separate node. Keep Useless Instruments as a participant and laboratory, not a permanent gatekeeper.

**Question:** Can we build something that stays useful when its original builders step away?
